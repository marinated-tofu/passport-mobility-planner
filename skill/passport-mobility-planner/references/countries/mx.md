## Benefits from visas and residence permits you already hold

### Visa-free entry - mx-entry-with-residence

- Purpose: tourism, business, transit
- Applies only if every check is yes. Write each check and its answer in your reply; one no means this rule does not apply.
  1. Does the traveller hold a residence permit issued by Canada, United States, Japan, United Kingdom, any Schengen state? It must meet: permanent residence card, valid at entry; any nationality. Its expiry must be after the last day of the stay; write the expiry date.
- Partial rule: if it applies, tell the traveller to confirm it on the source below.
- Notes (if the rule applies, copy every form to fill and every caveat into the reply): Permanent residents of Canada, the US, Japan, the UK or a Schengen country do not need a Mexican visa, whatever their nationality. Carry the residence card with the passport and fill in the FMMd. Visitors cannot switch to temporary or permanent residence inside Mexico except on humanitarian or family-unity grounds. No stay limit is given for this group on the consulate page.

### Visa-free entry - mx-entry-with-us-visa

- Purpose: tourism, business, transit
- Applies only if every check is yes. Write each check and its answer in your reply; one no means this rule does not apply.
  1. Does the traveller hold a visa issued by United States? It must meet: US visa must be valid (unexpired) at entry; any nationality. Its expiry must be after the last day of the stay; write the expiry date.
- Partial rule: if it applies, tell the traveller to confirm it on the source below.
- Notes (if the rule applies, copy every form to fill and every caveat into the reply): Travellers of any nationality who would normally need a Mexican visa can skip it if they carry a current US visa. A completed digital multiple migration form (FMMd) is still required, the passport must stay valid for the whole visit, and officers may ask for proof of the trip's purpose. The consulate page gives no stay limit for this group. Other Mexican consulates describe the rule differently (some also accept Canadian, Japanese, UK or Schengen visas, some require a multiple-entry visa that was already used), so confirm with the consulate that covers you.
