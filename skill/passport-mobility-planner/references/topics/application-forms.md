# Application forms and strict checks

Some consulates ask for far more than a passport and a booking: histories on the form, invitation letters with set fields, fingerprints, and steps after arrival. Use this file when a traveller applies for a visa or permit (not a visa-free entry or an online authorisation). Check only what the research excerpt names. One post's document list does not hold at another post or for another country: use the excerpt for the office the person applies at.

## Where and when to apply

- Many consulates take applications only from people who live legally in their country, at the office whose district covers their address. Compare the residence permit with the submission date, and the address with the district the excerpt names.
- Test passport validity and free visa pages on the submission date and again on the entry date.
- Some forms must be used, or travel must start, within a number of days of submission: put the earliest submission date in the calendar.

## Passports and earlier visas

- Some forms ask for every passport held. Nationality is judged under the destination's own law: a former national, or a child born abroad to a national, may need that country's travel document instead of a visa, decided by dates the excerpt names (for example a parent's permanent residence date against the child's birth date). Ask; never decide a nationality.
- Earlier visas for the same country: look in old passports on the NAS. A valid visa in an expired passport often counts only if the name, sex, birth date and nationality are unchanged. The first application after a name change needs the document that changed it.

## Histories the form asks for

Forms can ask for years of work, months of travel, every family member (also those not travelling), education, and military, government or media posts. Draft them from the documents only, for the person to check:

- work: employers with their full names and the dates from contracts, letters and payslips; months not covered in the period the form asks for are "to fill in";
- travel: countries and dates from visas, stamps and bookings, marked "from the documents, may be incomplete";
- family: everyone on the marriage and birth certificates, with only what the NAS shows.

Yes/no questions on refusals, overstays, criminal records and health: never answer them. List them under Left for you. A refusal letter or police record in the folder that a "no" would contradict is flagged.

## Invitations and category documents

Check each field the excerpt lists for the letter (the inviter's name, ID or permit number, address and phone; the invitee's name, birth date, nationality and passport number; dates and places; relationship; who pays; signature, seal and date). Flag a letter that quotes an old passport number or dates other than the form's. The relationship must be one the category allows, and a child's age is counted on the date the excerpt names. Each category's own document (work permit notice, admission letter, onward ticket, hotel and return booking) comes from the excerpt.

## Photos and fingerprints

Photo size, background and age from the excerpt. Fingerprints are given in person unless an exemption in the excerpt applies (age on the submission date, prints given at the same office within a period): put the appointment in the calendar.

## After arrival

- Every night needs a hotel booking or a host's address. Where the excerpt says guests outside hotels register with the police within some hours, put it in the calendar with the host's name.
- Arrival cards, a residence permit within some days of entry, extensions some days before expiry, reporting a change within some days: each from the excerpt, counted from the arrival date.

## Absences

For permanent residence, citizenship, re-entry and renewals, test each trip against any per-trip limit (for example a trip longer than six months) as well as the total in the period, counted the way the excerpt says, including trips after filing. Use only dates the person gives.

## Stop rule

False or inconsistent information can mean refusal and a ban. A document that looks altered, a name that does not match, or an answer the documents contradict stops the check for that person: say what to fix first.
