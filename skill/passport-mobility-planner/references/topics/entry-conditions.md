# Entry conditions

General patterns used by the plan route. They are not country rules; each country's own page decides.

## Passport validity

Countries usually set one of these minimums, each counted from an anchor date:

- valid for the whole stay;
- valid some months beyond the departure from the country, or from a shared visa area (the exit from the whole area, not from the first country);
- valid some months beyond the arrival date, or beyond the application date;
- valid for the permitted stay plus a number of days.

Compute the dates for the traveller and say which ones the passport meets. Which one a country applies comes from the research answer for that country; without one, say the rule was not found. When an excerpt gives a period without saying from which date, say so instead of picking one.

A rule on when the passport was issued (for example less than ten years before arrival) is a separate check: use the issue date printed on the passport, never work it out from the expiry date.

## The passport itself

- Some rules cover only some passports: electronic (chip symbol), machine-readable, ordinary rather than diplomatic, service, official, emergency or temporary, or one that shows a nationality status, a personal ID number or the place of birth. Compare the data page with the excerpt's words; when the scan cannot show a feature, write "check on the passport".
- Free visa pages do not show on the data page: ask, or list them under You confirm.
- A visa or travel authorisation belongs to the passport it was issued on. Compare its passport number (masked) with the passport the person travels on. A new passport usually needs a new authorisation, and a visa in an old passport means carrying both, unless the excerpt says otherwise.

## Travel authorisations

- Valid until its fixed term or the passport's expiry, whichever comes first; check it on the date the excerpt names (arrival, or every day of the stay).
- A change of name, nationality or answers can need a new one.
- Schemes start, pause and are waived for some nationalities: use the excerpt's status and date, never memory.
- Age limits are counted on the date the excerpt names, often the entry date.
- A visa, residence or nationality of the destination can replace it or rule it out; some apply only to arrivals by air.

## Shared visa areas

Some visas count for a group of countries (the Schengen area is one). Which countries are members, and what a shared visa covers, comes from the research answer; ask for it with the countries on the route. A visa with limited territorial validity covers only the countries printed on it.

## Other conditions travellers are often asked for

- free visa pages, often one or two;
- a return or onward ticket;
- proof of accommodation, sometimes booked through a named site or office;
- proof of funds;
- travel or health insurance;
- an invitation, sometimes an original that must reach the traveller before entry;
- an arrival card or health declaration filed within some hours before arrival;
- a vaccination asked for because of the route taken, not only the nationality;
- entry only at listed airports or ports, or only by air;
- a passport that is not damaged.

The documents on the NAS can show some of these (ticket, insurance, bank statement) when the user keeps them in the documents folder. Report what was found and what was not; never assume.

## A visa or permit from another country

Some countries accept a visa or residence from another country instead of their own visa, or as a condition for an easier one. Check each point the excerpt names: which document types and issuing countries count, the date it must be valid on (the application date or the travel date), any rule on when it was issued, whether it must have been used, whether an electronic one counts, and whether it must sit in the passport used. A point the excerpt does not cover is "not stated", never assumed.

## Transit

From the excerpt for each connection: whether the traveller stays airside or passes the border, the hours between flights, the airport and terminal, whether the next flight goes to a third country (a trip from one country through another and back to the first may not count as transit), and the airline when the excerpt names one. A transit visa can be needed even airside.

## The residence permit the traveller lives on

When the traveller holds a residence permit for the country they leave from or return to:

- it must be valid on the return date: compare its expiry with the last day of the trip, and when it expires before or within a month after, put renewing it first, before any visa application that relies on it;
- applying for a visa from a country of residence: consulates usually take applications only from legal residents, and many want the permit valid for some months beyond the return or the visa; the research answer says how many; without it, say the margin was not found;
- a permit can lapse after a long time outside the country, or need a re-entry permit: for a trip of more than a month, ask the research for the absence limit of the permit held.

## Where and when to apply

- Shared visa area: apply to the country of the main destination (the longest stay, or the main purpose when stays are equal), or else the country of first entry; say which one and why.
- The excerpt's earliest and latest dates before travel to apply go into the calendar file; a start-by date earlier than the earliest allowed date becomes that date.
- Dates of stay start on the local landing date: a flight that lands after midnight starts the stay the next day.

## Earlier stays, refusals and overstays

Some visa-free entries and visas allow a number of days within a period, so earlier stays count. Use the excerpt's way of counting: a rolling period, a fixed period from the first entry, or a calendar year; and whether the first day counted is the day of entry or the next day. How long an authorisation is valid is not how long each stay may be. Days spent on a residence permit or long-stay visa of the area usually do not count. An entry and exit system may not hold stays from before it started.

The NAS does not show entry stamps reliably: when an excerpt counts earlier stays, ask the traveller for their stays in that country or area within the period, count the days, or write "earlier stays not counted".

## What the documents cannot show

Dual nationals of the destination usually must use that country's passport. Visits to listed countries since a date, a parent's or grandparent's birthplace, a criminal record, and earlier refusals, overstays or removals can rule out a scheme. Never guess these: when an excerpt names one, ask once or list it under You confirm, and list form questions about them under Left for you.

## Names

Airlines and border officers compare the name on the ticket and visa with the passport. Report any difference, including a missing middle name or a changed surname noted on the observations page.
