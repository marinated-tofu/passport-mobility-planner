## China

### Visa-free transit - cn-24-hour-visa-free-transit

- Stay: up to 24 hours
- Purpose: direct transit to a third country or region
- Unlocked by, all of:
  - passport; any nationality; valid international travel document
  - ticket; through ticket with a confirmed seat on an international flight, ship or train to a third country or region, leaving within 24 hours
- Notes: Free and open to all nationalities at every open exit-entry port. The traveller must stay in the port's restricted area; going into the city needs a temporary entry permit from the port's border inspection office.
- Source: https://en.nia.gov.cn/n147418/n147463/c183412/content.html (National Immigration Administration (NIA), People's Republic of China)

### Visa-free transit - cn-240-hour-visa-free-transit

- Stay: up to 240 hours (10 days)
- Purpose: tourism, business, visits, family reunions while in transit to a third country or region
- Unlocked by, all of:
  - passport issued by Albania, Austria, Belarus, Belgium, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Monaco, Montenegro, Netherlands, North Macedonia, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Russian Federation, Serbia, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Ukraine, United Kingdom, Canada, United States, Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Mexico, Australia, New Zealand, Brunei Darussalam, Indonesia, Japan, Kyrgyzstan, Qatar, Singapore, South Korea, United Arab Emirates, Vietnam; ordinary passport, valid international travel document
  - ticket; confirmed onward ticket (seat and date fixed) to a third country or region, departing within 240 hours
- Notes: Free; no visa needed. 57 nationalities (Kyrgyzstan and Vietnam added 20 Aug 2026). Enter and leave through any of 65 designated open ports in 24 provinces and stay only inside the designated areas; the NIA page shows the port and area list as an image, so ports are not listed here. The onward trip must go to a third country or region, so a round trip back to the departure country does not qualify. Shanghai's government page says the clock starts at 00:00 on the day after arrival. Work, study and journalism still need a visa. Approval is decided by border inspection on arrival.
- Source: https://en.nia.gov.cn/n147418/n147463/c183412/content.html (National Immigration Administration (NIA), People's Republic of China)

## Hong Kong

### Visa-free transit - hk-airside-direct-transit-visa-nationals

- Stay: airside only, no entry
- Purpose: direct transit by air without leaving the airport transit area
- Unlocked by, all of:
  - passport issued by Azerbaijan, Cambodia, Cuba, Kyrgyzstan, Laos, Lebanon, Moldova, Myanmar, Nepal, Palestine, State of, Senegal, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Vietnam, Albania, Serbia, Tonga, Tuvalu; nationality that needs a visa to visit Hong Kong but is not flagged as needing a visa even airside; for AL and RS this covers the passport types that need a visa (non-biometric ones), for TO and TV the listed passport types; stateless document holders are also covered
  - ticket; onward flight in direct transit, staying inside the airport transit area
- Not for nationals of: Afghanistan, Central African Republic, Congo, The Democratic Republic of the, Côte d'Ivoire, Eritrea, Eswatini, Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, Iran, Iraq, North Korea, Liberia, Libya, Nigeria, Sierra Leone, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Syria, Yemen, Angola, Bangladesh, Burundi, Cameroon, Congo, Ethiopia, Ghana, Rwanda, Sri Lanka, Togo, Pakistan
- Notes: Hong Kong's rule is that visa nationals still pass airside without a visa when they connect by air and never leave the transit area. Nationals marked on ImmD's list (the excluded codes here) need a Hong Kong visa even airside; for several of them diplomatic and official passports are exempt. Visitors who do enter must normally hold onward or return tickets unless heading to the Mainland or Macao. List updated August 2026.
- Source: https://www.immd.gov.hk/eng/services/visas/visit-transit/visit-visa-entry-permit.html (Immigration Department, Hong Kong SAR Government)

## Japan

### Visa-free transit - jp-shore-pass-port-of-call

- Stay: under 72 hours
- Purpose: short landing at the port of call while in transit (no paid activity)
- Unlocked by, all of:
  - passport; valid passport for the onward destination; foreign passenger (not crew)
  - ticket; ticket for the trip out of Japan (or a carrier's letter of guarantee), leaving from the same port
- Notes: Landing permission at a port of call (often called a shore pass). Granted by an immigration inspector on written application; movement is generally limited to the city or town of the arrival port and paid work is banned. It is a discretionary permission, not an automatic right. Based on the Enforcement Regulation of the Immigration Control Act (official English translation); the day-to-day process at airports is not described there.
- Source: https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/en/laws/view/4180/en (Ministry of Justice, Japan (Japanese Law Translation database))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### Visa-free transit - jp-transit-pass-landing-in-transit

- Stay: up to 3 days
- Purpose: crossing Japan to board a departing flight or ship at another port (no paid activity)
- Unlocked by, all of:
  - passport; valid passport for the onward destination; foreign passenger (not crew)
  - ticket; ticket for the trip out of Japan (or a carrier's letter of guarantee) from a different port than the arrival port
- Notes: Landing permission in transit (often called a transit pass). Lets a passenger travel overland to another Japanese port to leave by plane or ship, up to 3 days, along a set route. A separate 15-day version exists for ship passengers rejoining their own vessel at another port. Discretionary; granted by an immigration inspector on written application.
- Source: https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/en/laws/view/4180/en (Ministry of Justice, Japan (Japanese Law Translation database))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

- Purpose: transit to or from a third country
- Unlocked by, all of:

## South Korea

### Visa-free transit - kr-chinese-group-jeju-transfer

- Stay: 15 days (B-2), with at most 5 days in the mainland travel region before going to Jeju
- Purpose: group tourism en route to Jeju
- Unlocked by, all of:
  - passport issued by China; travelling in a tour group of more than 3 people from mainland China, Hong Kong or Macau, booked through designated Korean and Chinese travel agencies
  - ticket; confirmed domestic flight or ferry ticket to Jeju leaving within 5 days
- Only for nationals of: China
- Notes: Chinese group tourists heading to Jeju may enter visa-free at Incheon, Gimpo, Gimhae, Cheongju, Muan, Daegu or Yangyang and tour a set region around that airport first. Ferries count only if they leave from a port inside that region. Tour guide supervision applies; no arrival card needed. May 2023 notice; not reconfirmed on a 2026 page.

## United Kingdom

### Visa-free transit - gb-airside-stateless-or-diplomatic

- Stay: airside, same day
- Purpose: airside transit without a DATV
- Unlocked by, all of:
  - passport; travel document of a person recognised as stateless under the 1954 UN Convention
  - ticket; arrive and depart by air, with a confirmed onward flight leaving the same day from the same airport
- Notes: Recognised stateless persons do not need a DATV to transit airside. Separately, the carrier list footnotes let diplomatic or service passport holders of several countries transit airside without a visa.
- Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-visa-requirements-list-for-carriers/uk-visa-requirements-for-international-carriers (UK Visas and Immigration, Home Office)

### Visa-free transit - gb-airside-twov-us-ca-au-nz-visa

- Stay: airside, same day
- Purpose: airside transit without a Direct Airside Transit Visa (DATV)
- Unlocked by, all of:
  - passport; a UK visa nationality that would otherwise need a visa or DATV to transit airside
  - visa issued by Australia, Canada, New Zealand, United States; valid entry visa for Australia, Canada, New Zealand or the USA (need not be travelling there); or an expired one if returning from that country within 6 months of last entry on it; a US B1/B2 visa is not accepted for some nationalities per the carrier list footnotes

- Notes: Free exemption from the DATV. Airside transit cannot be used to reach Ireland or the rest of the Common Travel Area. Other countries' e-visas only count if the airline can verify them. The final decision is made by a UK border officer. GOV.UK carrier list updated 3 Aug 2026.

### Visa-free transit - gb-landside-twov-us-ca-au-nz

- Stay: until 23:59 the day after arrival
- Purpose: landside transit (passing the UK border, e.g. to change airports)
- Unlocked by, all of:
  - passport; a UK visa nationality
  - visa issued by Australia, Canada, New Zealand, United States; valid visa for that country and travelling via the UK on a reasonable route to or from it; or permanent residence from AU, CA (after 28 June 2002), NZ or US; an EEA or Swiss residence permit or D visa also qualifies
  - ticket; arrive and depart by air, with a confirmed onward flight leaving before 23:59 the following day
- Notes: Free landside transit without a visa. Unlike the airside exemption, a US/CA/AU/NZ visa only counts when the trip is actually to or from that country. Other countries' e-visas or e-permits are not accepted for landside transit. A UK border officer decides on arrival.
- Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-visa-requirements-list-for-carriers/uk-visa-requirements-for-international-carriers (UK Visas and Immigration, Home Office)
