## Steps in order

### 1. Set up your UKVI account and get access to your eVisa - gb-evisa-ukvi-account

- Category: residence-registration
- Who: Anyone with a UK visa or settlement status, including each dependant (partner, children)
- How: Check first that you do not already have a UKVI account, then create one, confirm your identity with the smartphone app and link the account to your eVisa. Access is usually immediate but can take up to 24 hours.
- Notes: eVisas replace physical biometric residence permits (BRPs). You need a phone number, an email address and either a valid passport plus your visa application number (GWF or UAN) or BRP number, or an expired BRP. An expired BRP can be used to set up access for 24 months after its printed expiry date or until 31 December 2026, whichever is sooner. If you already replaced a BRP or applied for a visa online you probably have an account already; do not create a second one. Not needed for visitors travelling on an ETA.
- Needs: passport, residence-permit, other
- Source: https://www.gov.uk/get-access-evisa (UK Visas and Immigration (GOV.UK))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 2. Apply for a National Insurance number - gb-national-insurance-number

- Category: tax-number
- Who: People living in the UK who have the right to work and are working, looking for work or have a job offer
- How: Apply online while you are in the UK and prove your identity during the application, ideally by uploading a photo of yourself holding your passport. Allow up to 4 weeks after your identity is proved.
- Notes: Check your eVisa in your UKVI account (or the back of an old BRP) first: you may already have a number, and it stays the same for life. You can start work before the number arrives if you can prove your right to work. If you cannot upload photos you may need an appointment or to post copies of documents. Helpline (England, Scotland, Wales): 0800 141 2079.
- Needs: passport, residence-permit
- Source: https://www.gov.uk/apply-national-insurance-number/how-to-apply (HM Revenue & Customs (GOV.UK))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### 3. Prove your right to rent and know your rights as a tenant (England) - gb-right-to-rent

- Category: housing
- Who: Non-British, non-Irish adults renting a home in England
- How: Before a tenancy starts, give the landlord a share code from the Home Office online service (linked to your eVisa) or your original immigration documents. You do not need to prove your right to rent in Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland.
- Notes: Under the Renters' Rights Act, new and existing private tenancies in England became assured periodic tenancies from 1 May 2026 (no fixed terms). Landlords and agents may ask for no more than one month's rent in advance, must publish an asking rent and cannot accept bids above it, cannot refuse tenants for having children or receiving benefits, and can raise rent only once a year with two months' notice (challengeable at the First-tier Tribunal). Tenants can leave with two months' notice. Summary from the government Guide to the Renters' Rights Act: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act . The older How to rent guide was withdrawn on 1 May 2026.
- Needs: residence-permit
- Source: https://www.gov.uk/prove-right-to-rent (Home Office (GOV.UK))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### 4. Check your deposit is protected in a government-approved scheme - gb-tenancy-deposit-protection

- Category: housing
- Who: Tenants on an assured periodic tenancy who pay a deposit
- Deadline: Landlord must protect the deposit within 30 days of receiving it
- How: Ask the landlord or agent which scheme holds your deposit and check it on that scheme's website. At the end of the tenancy the deposit must be returned within 10 days of you and the landlord agreeing the amount; disputes are handled by the scheme.
- Notes: Approved schemes in England and Wales: Deposit Protection Service, MyDeposits and Tenancy Deposit Scheme. Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own schemes. Holding deposits do not need protecting until the tenancy starts. Under the Renters' Rights Act a landlord who has not protected the deposit generally cannot get a possession order. This page does not state a cap on deposit amounts.
- Needs: tenancy-contract
- Source: https://www.gov.uk/tenancy-deposit-protection (Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government (GOV.UK))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### 5. Register with an NHS GP surgery - gb-nhs-gp-registration

- Category: health-insurance
- Who: Everyone living in England (visitors can register as temporary patients)
- Fee: Free
- How: Find a GP surgery on the NHS website and register online (about 15 minutes per person), through the surgery's own form, or on a paper form taken to the surgery. You usually hear back within 5 days.
- Notes: You do not need proof of address, ID or immigration status to register. Most visa holders already paid the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) with their visa application, which lets them use the NHS free from the visa start date: 1,035 GBP per year, or 776 GBP per year for students and their dependants, under-18s and Youth Mobility Scheme applicants (https://www.gov.uk/healthcare-immigration-application/how-much-pay). Prescriptions, dental treatment, eye tests and assisted conception are still charged. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland run their own GP registration.
- Source: https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/gps/how-to-register-with-a-gp-surgery/ (NHS England)
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### 6. Open a bank account (basic bank account if refused a standard one) - gb-basic-bank-account

- Category: bank-account
- Who: People legally resident in the UK without a bank account or not eligible for a standard current account
- Fee: Fee-free for standard operations in sterling
- How: Ask one of the nine designated providers for a basic bank account if you cannot get a standard current account. Banks still run credit and financial crime checks when you open it.
- Notes: Designated providers that must offer basic bank accounts under the Payment Accounts Regulations 2015: Barclays UK, The Co-operative Bank, HSBC UK, Lloyds Banking Group (incl. Halifax, Bank of Scotland), Nationwide, NatWest Group (incl. RBS, Ulster Bank), Santander UK, TSB, Virgin Money UK. No overdraft. The page does not list the ID or address documents banks ask for.
- Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/basic-bank-accounts-july-2023-to-june-2024/basic-bank-accounts-july-2023-to-june-2024 (HM Treasury (GOV.UK))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 7. Exchange your foreign driving licence or plan to take a UK test - gb-driving-licence-exchange

- Category: driving-licence
- Who: Residents of Great Britain driving on a non-GB car licence
- Deadline: Drive on your foreign licence for up to 12 months after becoming resident; exchange (designated countries) within 5 years
- Fee: 43 GBP
- How: If your licence is from a designated country, get form D1 from a Post Office that offers DVLA services and post it with your licence and fee to the address on the form; it usually takes about 3 weeks. If your country is not designated, apply for a GB provisional licence and pass the theory and practical tests before your 12 months run out.
- Notes: Designated countries and territories (car licences): Andorra, Australia, Barbados, British Virgin Islands, Canada, Cayman Islands, Falkland Islands, Faroe Islands, Gibraltar, Hong Kong, Japan, Monaco, New Zealand, Republic of Korea, Moldova, North Macedonia, Singapore, South Africa, Switzerland, Taiwan, Ukraine, UAE, Zimbabwe. Some licences (e.g. several Australian states) need proof you passed in a manual car, otherwise you get automatic-only. Use the GOV.UK tool for your exact country; for other countries see https://www.gov.uk/driving-nongb-licence . Northern Ireland has its own process on nidirect. Fee and steps shown are for an Australian licence on the tool.
- Needs: application-form, other
- Source: https://www.gov.uk/exchange-nongb-driving-licence/y/yes/car-or-motorcycle/a-designated-country-or-territory-countries-or-territories-with-exchange-agreements-with-great-britain/australia (Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency (GOV.UK))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### 8. Register as self-employed (sole trader) for Self Assessment - gb-self-employed-registration

- Category: business-registration
- Who: People earning more than 1,000 GBP from self-employment in a tax year (6 April to 5 April)
- Deadline: By 5 October after the end of the tax year you first need to file for (5 October 2026 for the 2025 to 2026 tax year)
- How: Check whether you need to send a tax return, then register online with HMRC for Self Assessment; you will receive a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) and file yearly returns.
- Notes: Registering after the deadline can lead to a penalty. Check your visa conditions allow self-employment before you start trading; many work visas do not. Sole trader overview: https://www.gov.uk/set-up-sole-trader
- Source: https://www.gov.uk/register-for-self-assessment/self-employed (HM Revenue & Customs (GOV.UK))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### 9. Register a limited company with Companies House - gb-companies-house-limited-company

- Category: business-registration
- Who: Anyone setting up a private limited company in the UK
- Fee: 100 GBP online (usually registered within 24 hours); 124 GBP by post on form IN01 (8 to 10 days)
- How: Register online with a registered office address, at least one director and shareholder, a SIC code and details of people with significant control. You will usually be set up for Corporation Tax at the same time.
- Notes: Directors may need to verify their identity through GOV.UK One Login and get a personal code. The company needs its own Government Gateway user ID; a personal one cannot be used. Check your visa conditions allow you to run a business.
- Source: https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/register-your-company (Companies House (GOV.UK))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

## Local customs

- alcohol: Drink-drive limits are stricter in Scotland (22 micrograms per 100 ml of breath) than in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (35). The government says there is no reliable number of drinks that keeps you under the limit, because it depends on weight, sex, food and other factors. Source: https://www.gov.uk/drink-drive-limit (GOV.UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- alcohol: It is illegal to sell alcohol to anyone under 18 or for an adult to buy it for them, and police can stop, fine or arrest under-18s drinking in public. Source: https://www.gov.uk/alcohol-young-people-law (GOV.UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- drugs-and-medicines: Drugs are split into classes A, B and C; possession can mean up to 7, 5 or 2 years in prison respectively, plus unlimited fines. Cannabis, ketamine and codeine are Class B, and nitrous oxide is Class C. Source: https://www.gov.uk/penalties-drug-possession-dealing (Home Office (GOV.UK)), checked 07 Nov 2026
- lgbt: The Equality Act 2010 protects people from discrimination on grounds including sexual orientation, gender reassignment, race, religion or belief, sex and age, at work, in housing, education and services. Source: https://www.gov.uk/discrimination-your-rights (GOV.UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- public-behaviour: Carrying a knife in public without a good reason is a crime with up to 4 years in prison; only small non-locking folding pocketknives with a blade of 3 inches or less are exempt. Many weapons, such as knuckledusters, flick knives and zombie knives, are banned outright. Source: https://www.gov.uk/buying-carrying-knives (GOV.UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- public-behaviour: Smoking is banned in enclosed workplaces, public buildings, public transport and shared work vehicles across the UK, with fines for individuals and businesses. The ban does not cover e-cigarettes, which employers regulate themselves. Source: https://www.gov.uk/smoking-at-work-the-law (GOV.UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- work-culture: Workers generally cannot be made to work more than 48 hours a week averaged over 17 weeks unless they choose to opt out; under-18s are limited to 8 hours a day and 40 a week. Source: https://www.gov.uk/maximum-weekly-working-hours (GOV.UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
