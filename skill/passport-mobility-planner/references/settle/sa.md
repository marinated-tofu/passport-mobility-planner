### Premium Residency (entrepreneur) - sa-premium-residency-entrepreneur

- Purpose: residence for founders of funded start-ups
- Fee: SAR 4,000 one-time
- Minimum investment: Category 1: funding of at least SAR 400,000 from a centre-accredited entity and at least 20% ownership; Category 2: at least SAR 15 million raised, at least 10% ownership and 10 new jobs in each of the first two years
- Validity: Category 2 permanent, conditional on job creation; Category 1 term not stated
- Stay: No minimum stay stated
- How to apply: Online via the Premium Residency Center portal (pr.gov.sa)
- Eligibility: Entrepreneur licence from the Ministry of Investment and a recommendation letter from the investing entity
- Proof of funds: Evidence of the funding round from an accredited investor

## Steps in order

### 1. Make sure your employer issues your work permit on Qiwa - sa-work-permit

- Category: residence-registration
- Who: Expatriate employees of private establishments; the employer applies
- Deadline: Within the first 90 days after the worker enters the Kingdom
- Fee: SAR 100 (as listed on the service page), paid through Sadad
- How: Your employer issues the work permit through the Qiwa portal. A valid work permit is a condition for issuing your residence permit (iqama).
- Notes: The service page lists no documents from the worker. After payment the iqama can be issued by the Passports office; payment status usually reaches the passport systems within 24 hours.
- Source: https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/en/ministry-services/services/%D8%A5%D8%B5%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%88-%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF-%D8%B1%D8%AE%D8%B5-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%85%D9%84 (Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (HRSD))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### 2. Get health insurance through your employer for you and your family - sa-health-insurance

- Category: health-insurance
- Who: Expatriate workers and accompanying family members
- Deadline: Before the iqama is issued
- How: Your employer provides health insurance for you and any accompanying family members; it is one of the conditions for issuing the iqama.
- Notes: Taken from the iqama conditions in the Absher Business guide. The Council of Health Insurance page (chi.gov.sa) could not be loaded on 2026-11-07, so cover rules are not confirmed here.
- Source: https://www.absher.sa/wps/wcm/connect/business/afd5e859-82e7-440b-b8c5-8894b040d988/book1_Bus-V6_en.pdf?MOD=AJPERES&CONVERT_TO=url&CACHEID=ROOTWORKSPACE.Z18_GPHIG0G0LOC770ARKN38H328B4-afd5e859-82e7-440b-b8c5-8894b040d988-nv3gmCN (Absher, Ministry of Interior, Saudi Arabia)
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 3. Pass the medical exam, enrol fingerprints and get your iqama - sa-iqama

- Category: residence-registration
- Who: Expatriate workers and their family members, issued by the employer
- How: Pass the medical examination at an accredited medical centre and enrol your fingerprints and photo. Your employer then issues the iqama online through Absher Business and it is delivered by Saudi Post within 3 to 5 working days.
- Notes: Conditions: you must be inside the Kingdom, your passport must be valid, iqama fees and any delay penalty and traffic fines must be paid, health insurance must be in place, and you must not be marked absent from work. The guide is a 2021 edition and does not state the fee.
- Needs: passport, medical-certificate, health-insurance
- Source: https://www.absher.sa/wps/wcm/connect/business/afd5e859-82e7-440b-b8c5-8894b040d988/book1_Bus-V6_en.pdf?MOD=AJPERES&CONVERT_TO=url&CACHEID=ROOTWORKSPACE.Z18_GPHIG0G0LOC770ARKN38H328B4-afd5e859-82e7-440b-b8c5-8894b040d988-nv3gmCN (Absher, Ministry of Interior, Saudi Arabia)
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 4. Register and activate your Absher account - sa-absher-account

- Category: identity
- Who: Citizens, residents and visitors
- How: Register on Absher, then activate the account through a bank account, a self-service kiosk, the Nafath app or a passports and civil affairs office. After activation you can log in on the website or the Absher app.
- Notes: The page does not list the identity details a resident must enter; residents normally register with their iqama.
- Needs: residence-permit
- Source: https://www.absher.sa/wps/portal/individuals/static/footer/registerinabsher/?uri=nm:oid:absher.footer.subscribe (Absher, Ministry of Interior, Saudi Arabia)
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 5. Have your residential lease registered on Ejar - sa-ejar-lease

- Category: housing
- Who: Tenants renting a home
- How: Residential leases are registered on Ejar, the Real Estate General Authority's rental platform, using the unified lease contract. If the landlord refuses to register, the tenant can ask for registration on Ejar; the landlord then has sixty days to object to the contract details.
- Notes: Riyadh rents inside the urban boundary are frozen for five years from 25 September 2025; elsewhere increases follow the contract. Leases renew automatically, and a landlord must give at least 60 days' notice of non-renewal for permitted reasons only. The page does not state fees or whether registration is mandatory. Rental transaction data: https://rega.gov.sa/en/rega-services/platforms/real-estate-indicators/ . Ejar help centre 199011.
- Needs: tenancy-contract
- Source: https://rega.gov.sa/en/rega-services/platforms/ejar/ (Real Estate General Authority (REGA), Saudi Arabia)
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 6. Open a bank account - sa-bank-account

- Category: bank-account
- Who: Natural persons, including residents (iqama holders)
- How: Present your original ID and sign the bank's account-opening agreement giving your nationality, ID number and expiry, national address, occupation, contact details and source of income. Banks must open the account within one business day if all requirements are met.
- Notes: The SAMA rule asks for an original ID without naming the iqama; the remote-opening rules cover residents who hold an iqama. You must give the bank a renewed ID before the old one expires or the account can be frozen.
- Needs: residence-permit, application-form
- Source: https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/2-documents-required-open-bank-account-are-follows (Saudi Central Bank (SAMA))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 7. Make sure your mobile and data SIMs are authenticated under your ID - sa-sim-authentication

- Category: phone
- Who: Anyone with numbers registered under their ID
- How: Use the CST Inquire about my numbers service with your ID number to see the authenticated numbers registered to you. Numbers that still need authentication do not appear; contact your provider to authenticate them so they are not cancelled.
- Notes: A 2016 CST notice says fingerprint verification at provider outlets is free (cst.gov.sa/en/media-center/news/CITC-Fingerprint-Documentation-Service-is-Free-and-a-15-Day-Grace-Period-for-Citizens-Residing-Abroa). Current authentication methods were not confirmed.
- Needs: residence-permit
- Source: https://www.cst.gov.sa/en/services/inquire-about-my-numbers (Communications, Space and Technology Commission (CST))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 8. Register your investment with the Ministry of Investment (MISA) - sa-misa-investment-registration

- Category: business-registration
- Who: Foreign investors setting up a company in an activity open to investment
- Fee: Set by the ministry on approval; pay within 15 business days of notification or the registration is void
- How: Apply through MISA's electronic services portal for investment registration. Estimated processing time is 10 working days.
- Notes: The foreign partner's commercial registration and last-year financial statements must be certified by the Saudi Embassy. Special Residency Permit holders are exempt from those documents. Activity-specific requirements apply. Amendments must then be reflected in the commercial register within 30 days.
- Needs: business-registration, other
- Source: https://misa.gov.sa/app/uploads/2026/09/Investor-Guide_13-03-en.pdf (Ministry of Investment, Saudi Arabia (MISA))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

## Local customs

- alcohol: Alcohol is banned: you cannot bring it in, drink it or be drunk in public, and making, holding or selling it can lead to prison. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/saudi-arabia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- dress: Both men and women are expected to wear loose clothing covering the elbows and knees, and to avoid tight clothes or slogans that could be seen as offensive or political. Women visitors do not have to wear an abaya or cover their hair. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/saudi-arabia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- drugs-and-medicines: Drug offences carry zero tolerance; even small amounts can mean up to 5 years in prison and a SAR 30,000 fine, and trafficking can carry the death penalty. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/saudi-arabia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- lgbt: Same-sex relations and sex outside marriage are illegal, although prosecution is uncommon; transgender identity is not recognised. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/saudi-arabia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- photography: Do not photograph government, diplomatic, military or palace buildings, and photograph people only with their consent. Do not share images of attacks or security incidents online. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/saudi-arabia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- public-behaviour: Public affection, swearing, rude gestures, loud music and public criticism of the royal family, government or religion can bring fines, prison or deportation. Always carry your iqama (the digital one on Tawakkalna or Absher is accepted). Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/saudi-arabia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- ramadan: During Ramadan daylight hours, do not eat, drink, smoke or chew gum in public (including in your car), and avoid loud music, dancing and swearing. Expect changed opening hours. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/saudi-arabia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- religion: Islam is the only religion that may be practised publicly, though private worship of other faiths is tolerated. Trying to convert others is illegal, and religious books are fine for personal use but not in large numbers. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/saudi-arabia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
