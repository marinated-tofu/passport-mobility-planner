## Long-term residence and citizenship routes

### Malaysia My Second Home (Gold) - my-mm2h-gold

- Document: residence-permit
- Purpose: long-term social visit pass
- Fee: MYR 3,000 one-off participating fee per principal; MYR 3,000 renewal fee
- Minimum investment: Fixed deposit of USD 500,000; residential purchase of MYR 1 million or above after approval
- Validity: 15 years, renewable; multiple-entry visa tied to passport validity
- Stay: 90 days cumulative per year; for participants aged 25 to 49 the principal or dependants can meet it
- How to apply: Through MM2H-licensed agents listed on mm2h.gov.my
- Eligibility: No age limit for the principal; same dependant rules as Silver
- Proof of funds: USD 500,000 fixed deposit; up to 50% may be withdrawn after approval
- For ranking: fee about USD 710, waiting days unknown, investment about USD 740,000
- Required documents: passport, passport-photo, application-form, medical-certificate, bank-statements, other
- Notes: The property cannot be sold for 10 years but can be swapped for a higher-value one.
- Source: https://www.mm2h.gov.my/category/gold (Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture Malaysia (MOTAC))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### Malaysia My Second Home (Platinum) - my-mm2h-platinum

- Document: residence-permit
- Purpose: long-term social visit pass with business and work rights
- Fee: MYR 200,000 one-off participation fee per principal; MYR 5,000 renewal fee

- Notes: The only main tier that allows business, investment and work.
- Source: https://www.mm2h.gov.my/category/platinum (Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture Malaysia (MOTAC))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### Malaysia My Second Home (SEZ/SFZ, Forest City Johor) - my-mm2h-sez-sfz

- Document: residence-permit
- Purpose: long-term social visit pass tied to the Forest City special zone
- Fee: MYR 1,000 one-off participation fee per principal; MYR 300 renewal fee
- Minimum investment: Fixed deposit of USD 65,000 (ages 21 to 49) or USD 32,000 (ages 50 and above); compulsory home purchase in Forest City, Johor, subject to the Johor floor price
- Validity: 10 years, renewable every 5 years; tied to passport validity
- Stay: 90 days cumulative per year
- How to apply: Through MM2H-licensed agents listed on mm2h.gov.my
- Eligibility: Principal must be 21 or older
- Proof of funds: Fixed deposit as above; up to 50% may be withdrawn after approval
- For ranking: fee about USD 240, waiting days unknown, investment about USD 32,000
- Required documents: passport, passport-photo, application-form, medical-certificate, bank-statements, other
- Notes: No business or employment allowed. The pass is revoked if the property condition lapses. USD minimum excludes the property, whose floor price is not stated.
- Source: https://www.mm2h.gov.my/category/sez (Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture Malaysia (MOTAC))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### Malaysia My Second Home (Silver) - my-mm2h-silver

- Document: residence-permit
- Purpose: long-term social visit pass
- Fee: MYR 1,000 one-off participating fee per principal; MYR 1,500 renewal fee
- Minimum investment: Fixed deposit of USD 150,000 in a licensed Malaysian institution; compulsory residential purchase of MYR 600,000 or above
- Validity: 5 years, renewable; multiple-entry visa tied to passport validity

- How to apply: Through MM2H-licensed agents listed on mm2h.gov.my

- For ranking: fee about USD 240, waiting days unknown, investment about USD 290,000
- Required documents: passport, passport-photo, application-form, medical-certificate, bank-statements, other
- Notes: No business or employment allowed. The property cannot be sold for 10 years. USD figure combines deposit and property minimum.

## Steps in order

### 1. Get your Employment Pass endorsed (ePASS) and collect your i-KAD - my-employment-pass-endorsement

- Category: residence-registration
- Who: Newly approved Employment Pass holders whose employer is registered with the Expatriate Services Division (ESD)
- How: Your employer submits and pays for the endorsement in ESD Online. Either go to the ESD counter at KLIA Terminal 1 or 2 on arrival with your passport and flight ticket, or enter through any port and your employer downloads the ePASS from ESD Online for you.
- Notes: The ePASS is processed one working day after payment and is valid only with the passport registered on it, so carry both. If you need a Visa with Reference, get it first. The i-KAD is collected at the airport counter or posted by Pos Malaysia to the address your employer chose. The FAQ does not state the endorsement fee, which the employer pays.
- Needs: passport, visa, flight-booking
- Source: https://esd.imi.gov.my/portal/faq/myxpats/ (Expatriate Services Division, Immigration Department of Malaysia)
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 2. Register your prepaid SIM card in person - my-prepaid-sim-registration

- Category: phone
- Who: All prepaid SIM users, including foreign workers and students
- How: Bring your original passport and work pass to the service provider's centre or an authorised dealer, where it is checked with a passport reader and facial recognition. You must register the SIM yourself.
- Notes: Photocopies, photos or scans are not accepted. Non-Malaysians may hold at most two prepaid SIMs per service provider. A SIM that is already active before you register must not be used; activation is confirmed within 12 hours of registration. Rules set by MCMC Determination No. 1 of 2026.
- Needs: passport, residence-permit
- Source: https://www.mcmc.gov.my/skmmgovmy/media/General/pdf2/FAQ_English_3.pdf (Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### 3. Open a bank account using your ePASS - my-bank-account

- Category: bank-account
- Who: Employment Pass holders with an ePASS
- How: Show your ePASS together with your passport at the bank.
- Notes: The ESD FAQ says the ePASS can be used to open a bank account but does not list any other bank requirements. No Bank Negara Malaysia page on foreigners' account opening was found.
- Needs: passport, residence-permit
- Source: https://esd.imi.gov.my/portal/faq/myxpats/ (Expatriate Services Division, Immigration Department of Malaysia)
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 4. Check the landlord's or agent's bank account and phone number before paying a deposit - my-rental-scam-check

- Category: housing
- Who: Anyone about to pay a booking fee, deposit or rent to a landlord or agent
- How: Enter the bank account number, phone number or company name you were given into the Semak Mule portal run by the police Commercial Crime Investigation Department. It shows whether they have been used in commercial crime cases.
- Notes: A clean result does not prove the listing is genuine. No official Malaysian page on tenancy rules or deposit limits was found (the housing ministry site kpkt.gov.my did not load).
- Source: https://semakmule.rmp.gov.my/ (Commercial Crime Investigation Department (CCID), Royal Malaysia Police)
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 5. Have your tenancy agreement stamped with LHDN - my-tenancy-stamp-duty

- Category: housing
- Who: Parties to a tenancy agreement signed in Malaysia
- Deadline: Within 30 days of signing in Malaysia, or within 30 days of receipt in Malaysia if signed abroad
- How: Stamp the signed agreement with LHDN, online through e-Duti Setem (STAMPS) or at an LHDN office, before the 30-day deadline.
- Notes: Late stamping from 1 January 2025 costs RM50 or 10% of the unpaid duty, whichever is higher, if within 3 months of the deadline, and RM100 or 20% after that. The penalty page does not give the duty rate for tenancy agreements or say who pays it. NAPIC (JPPH) publishes official property price and rental indices at https://napic.jpph.gov.my/archives/indeks-harga-dan-sewaan-harta-tanah.
- Needs: tenancy-contract
- Source: https://www.hasil.gov.my/en/duti-setem/penalti-duti-setem/ (Inland Revenue Board of Malaysia (LHDN / HASiL))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 6. Apply for an income tax number (TIN) through e-Daftar - my-tax-number

- Category: tax-number
- Who: Non-citizen individuals (file type IG); automatic registration covers only citizens and permanent residents aged 18 and over
- How: Apply online through e-Daftar on the MyTax portal and upload a copy of your valid passport. Online e-Daftar has been the only route for individuals since 1 January 2024.
- Notes: Foreigners carrying on a business must also provide their SSM registration certificate or the relevant agreement.
- Needs: passport
- Source: https://www.hasil.gov.my/en/individu/pendaftaran/ (Inland Revenue Board of Malaysia (LHDN / HASiL))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### 7. Check whether you may convert your foreign driving licence - my-driving-licence-conversion

- Category: driving-licence
- Who: Only Malaysian citizens, diplomatic identity card holders and MM2H participants; Employment Pass holders are not an eligible category
- Fee: RM20 processing fee and RM120 licence fee for a two-year probationary licence (PDL)
- How: Eligible applicants apply in person at a state JPJ office; applications are not accepted online. Approved applicants receive a probationary licence.
- Notes: Processing takes 30 working days. Holders of licences from countries outside the 1949 Convention must attend the KPP01 driver education course after approval. An International Driving Permit cannot be converted. The FAQ does not list the documents to bring.
- Source: https://www.jpj.gov.my/faq-pertukaran-lesen-memandu-luar-negara-08092026/ (Road Transport Department Malaysia (JPJ))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 8. Incorporate a private company (Sdn. Bhd.) with SSM - my-company-incorporation

- Category: business-registration
- Who: Anyone setting up a local company; a private company needs at least one director who ordinarily resides in Malaysia with a principal place of residence there
- Deadline: Appoint a company secretary within 30 days of incorporation
- Fee: RM1,000 incorporation fee plus RM10 data-sharing fee; RM50 for an optional name reservation
- How: Register as a user on the SSM4U portal and apply in the Corporate Registry System (CRS) by name reservation, direct registration or guaranteed incorporation. SSM aims to decide within one working day of a complete application with full payment.
- Notes: Fees are non-refundable. You must answer any query within 30 days or the application is rejected. A notice of registration does not replace licences or permits from other authorities. The guideline does not list identity documents.
- Source: https://ssm.com.my/Pages/Legal_Framework/GUIDELINES/Guidelines-For-Incorporation-of-A-Local-Company.pdf (Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

## Local customs

- alcohol: Police run regular breath tests, and drink-driving can lead to a heavy fine, prison or deportation. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/malaysia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- dress: Dress modestly, especially outside the big cities. Mosques, temples and government offices may turn you away unless your shoulders and knees are covered and you wear closed shoes. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/malaysia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- drugs-and-medicines: Any illegal drug, cannabis included, is treated with zero tolerance. Even tiny amounts can mean a long jail term or whipping, and trafficking can carry the death penalty. Police may require a urine or blood test in venues popular with foreigners. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/malaysia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- lgbt: Same-sex sexual acts are illegal under federal law, police sometimes raid LGBT+ venues and events, and public affection can draw hostile reactions. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/malaysia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- other: The law requires you to carry identification, so keep your passport on you. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/malaysia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- ramadan: During Ramadan, shop and restaurant hours change and modest dress matters even more; dates move each year. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/malaysia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
- religion: Malaysia is multicultural with a Muslim majority, and Muslims, including foreign Muslims, can fall under Sharia law as well as civil law. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/malaysia/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, UK), checked 07 Nov 2026
