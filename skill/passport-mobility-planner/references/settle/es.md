## Steps in order

### 1. Get your foreigner identity number (NIE) - es-nie

- Category: tax-number
- Who: Every foreign national with economic, professional or social dealings in Spain who has not yet been given one
- Fee: €9.84 (form 790 code 012)
- How: If you are abroad, apply at the Spanish consulate for your area; if you are legally in Spain, book an appointment online with the National Police or the immigration office in your province. Bring form EX-15 in duplicate, your passport, a document showing why you need the NIE, and proof that you paid the fee.
- Notes: The NIE is a number for life, not a residence or work permit, and it does not expire. Many residence-visa holders already have one; it then appears on the TIE card. Processing is about 5 days. Foreign documents must be translated by a sworn translator and apostilled or legalised. Sources in Barcelona City Council's guide; the procedure is national (Policía Nacional).
- Needs: passport, application-form, other
- Source: https://www.barcelona.cat/internationalwelcome/en/procedure/identity-number-foreign-nationals-white-nie (Ajuntament de Barcelona (Barcelona International Welcome))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### 2. Rent a home and know your tenant rights - es-rent-a-home

- Category: housing
- Who: Anyone renting a home as their main residence
- How: Sign a written tenancy contract. The legal deposit is one month's rent, and the landlord can ask for at most two more months as extra guarantee; agency and contract costs are paid by the landlord.
- Notes: Minimum term is 5 years with a private landlord or 7 with a company, then up to 3 years of tacit extension. You can leave with 30 days' notice before the end of the agreed term or a yearly extension. Yearly rent rises are capped by the IRAV index (calculator: https://www.mivau.gob.es/vivienda/calculadora-precio-alquiler). Check typical rents with the official SERPAVI rent reference index: https://serpavi.mivau.gob.es/ (overview: https://www.mivau.gob.es/vivienda/alquila-bien-es-tu-derecho/serpavi). Regional deposit bodies: https://www.mivau.gob.es/vivienda/alquila-bien-es-tu-derecho/alquiler/deposito-de-fianzas
- Needs: tenancy-contract
- Source: https://www.mivau.gob.es/vivienda/alquila-bien-es-tu-derecho/alquiler/10-preguntas (Ministerio de Vivienda y Agenda Urbana)
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### 3. Register at your town hall (empadronamiento / padrón) - es-empadronamiento

- Category: address-registration
- Who: Everyone who lives in a Spanish municipality
- Fee: Free
- How: Register with your town hall in person by appointment, online with a digital certificate, or (in Barcelona) by phone on 010. Bring your passport or TIE and proof of address such as your tenancy contract, a recent utility bill, or the owner's written authorisation.
- Notes: The padrón gives access to public healthcare and schooling and is required for the TIE. Non-EU nationals without long-term residence must renew it every 2 years. Documents not in Spanish or Catalan need a sworn translation. Source is Barcelona's guide; other town halls run the same register but may ask for different papers.
- Needs: passport, tenancy-contract
- Source: https://www.barcelona.cat/internationalwelcome/en/procedure/registration-municipal-register-residents-city-barcelona (Ajuntament de Barcelona (Barcelona International Welcome))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### 4. Get a Social Security number and affiliation - es-social-security-number

- Category: other
- Who: Anyone starting work in Spain, employed or self-employed
- Deadline: Before you start work
- How: Request it with form TA.1 at a Provincial Directorate of the Tesorería General de la Seguridad Social, or online at the Sede Electrónica with a digital certificate. Employers request affiliation for their staff; self-employed people apply themselves.
- Notes: Foreign nationals who live or are legally in Spain and work there are covered by the system. The page does not list supporting documents.
- Needs: application-form
- Source: https://www.seg-social.es/wps/portal/wss/internet/Trabajadores/Afiliacion/7332 (Tesorería General de la Seguridad Social)
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 5. Get your foreigner identity card (TIE) - es-tie

- Category: residence-registration
- Who: Non-EU nationals with a visa or residence permit for more than 6 months, and all long-term residents
- Deadline: Within one month of arriving in Spain or of the residence permit decision being published
- Fee: €16.08 for a first temporary residence card; €21.87 for a long-term residence card (form 790 code 012)
- How: Book a fingerprinting appointment at the police station in your province through the government appointment website, pay the fee first, then attend in person with form EX-17 (or MI-TIE for Entrepreneurs Act profiles), your passport with entry stamp, a recent colour photo on white background, your padrón certificate and the residence permit decision. Collect the card about 40 to 45 days later.
- Notes: The TIE is the physical card showing your photo, NIE and permit type; without it you can be fined even with a visa. Entrepreneurs Act profiles such as digital nomads do not need the padrón certificate. Bring originals and copies. Fees from the Policía form 790 code 012 (https://sede.policia.gob.es/Tasa790_012/ImpresoRellenar).
- Needs: passport, passport-photo, application-form, residence-permit, accommodation-proof
- Source: https://www.barcelona.cat/internationalwelcome/en/procedure/identity-card-foreign-nationals-tie (Ajuntament de Barcelona (Barcelona International Welcome))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, verified

### 6. Open a basic payment account - es-basic-payment-account

- Category: bank-account
- Who: People legally resident in the EU, even without a fixed address, who do not already hold a current account in Spain
- Fee: Up to €3 a month
- How: Ask any bank that offers payment accounts for a basic payment account (cuenta de pago básica). It can only refuse if you already have a current account in Spain, on national security or public order grounds, or if you do not give the information needed under anti-money-laundering rules.
- Notes: The monthly fee covers opening, use and closing, cash deposits and withdrawals, debit card payments and 120 EU payment transactions a year. Fee page: https://clientebancario.bde.es/pcb/es/menu-horizontal/productosservici/cuentasdepositos/cuentascorriente/guia-textual/CuentadepagoBasica/comisiones.html. The page does not list documents.
- Source: https://clientebancario.bde.es/pcb/es/menu-horizontal/productosservici/cuentasdepositos/cuentascorriente/guia-textual/CuentadepagoBasica/quienes-pueden-solicitarla-.html (Banco de España)
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 7. Register for Cl@ve (government digital login) - es-clave

- Category: identity
- Who: Holders of a DNI or NIE who want to use Spanish online public services
- How: Register online with your NIE and its support number, then confirm by video call or with the invitation letter posted to your tax address, or register in person at a registration office such as the tax agency or Social Security.
- Notes: You need a mobile number (one DNI or NIE per number; foreign numbers are allowed) and an email address. In-person registration cannot be done through a representative and many offices need an appointment. Fee not stated on the page.
- Needs: residence-permit
- Source: https://clave.gob.es/registro/como-puedo-registrarme/registro-basico-internet-carta-de-invitacion (Gobierno de España (Cl@ve))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 8. Exchange your foreign driving licence - es-driving-licence-exchange

- Category: driving-licence
- Who: Residents with a licence from a non-EU/EEA country that has an exchange agreement with Spain
- Deadline: Within 6 months of becoming normally resident in Spain
- How: Apply to the DGT with form Mod.03 and hand in your original foreign licence. If your country has no agreement with Spain, you must take a Spanish licence instead.
- Notes: Agreement countries include Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Japan, South Korea, Morocco, New Zealand, Peru, the Philippines, Switzerland, Ukraine, the UK and others listed on the page. A licence obtained while already legally resident in Spain after the agreement was signed cannot be exchanged. Information line 060. Fee not stated on the page.
- Needs: application-form, other
- Source: https://sede.dgt.gob.es/es/permisos-de-conducir/canjes-de-permisos/canjes-de-permisos-extranjeros/ (Dirección General de Tráfico (DGT))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

### 9. Register as self-employed (autónomo) with the tax agency - es-autonomo

- Category: business-registration
- Who: Foreign residents allowed to work who start a business or freelance activity
- How: File the census declaration Modelo 036 with the Agencia Tributaria; first-time entrepreneurs can use the Censos WEB online helper. Then request your own Social Security affiliation in the self-employed scheme (RETA).
- Notes: Foreign self-employed workers who live and work legally in Spain are covered by RETA. The page does not list documents or fees.
- Needs: application-form
- Source: https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/procedimientoini/G322.shtml (Agencia Estatal de Administración Tributaria (AEAT))
- Checked: 07 Nov 2026, partial

## Local customs

- alcohol: Drinking in the street is banned in some areas with on-the-spot fines. Parts of the Balearics (Magaluf, Playa de Palma, San Antonio) restrict happy hours, pub crawls and shop alcohol sales between 9:30pm and 8am. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/spain/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (UK)), checked 07 Nov 2026
- dress: Some towns ban walking the streets in swimwear or bare-chested, with fines. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/spain/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (UK)), checked 07 Nov 2026
- drugs-and-medicines: Drug offences, cannabis included, bring heavy penalties, even for travellers in transit. Some areas also fine people for using nitrous oxide. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/spain/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (UK)), checked 07 Nov 2026
- lgbt: Spain is generally tolerant; same-sex marriage is legal and legal gender change does not require surgery. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/spain/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (UK)), checked 07 Nov 2026
- other: Change money only at official outlets; passing counterfeit notes is a crime even if you did not know they were fake. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/spain/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (UK)), checked 07 Nov 2026
- public-behaviour: Carry photo ID and show it when any police force asks; refusing a direct police request can be a criminal offence. Hotels and car hire firms record your passport. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/spain/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (UK)), checked 07 Nov 2026
- public-behaviour: Rowdy balcony behaviour can lead to fines or eviction in some regions, and going to unlicensed villa parties in Ibiza and Mallorca can be fined. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/spain/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (UK)), checked 07 Nov 2026
- religion: You may be asked to remove a burka or niqab when entering some public buildings. Source: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/spain/safety-and-security (Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (UK)), checked 07 Nov 2026
