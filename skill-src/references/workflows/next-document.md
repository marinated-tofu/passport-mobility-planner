# Next document route

Answers "which visa, authorisation or residence permit should I get next to open up the most countries for the least money and waiting time".

## Inputs

- The person's holdings and today's unlocked benefits from the benefits route.
- Research, following research.md: one request with topic visas for the countries the user is interested in (ALL when they name none), and the question "visas, authorisations and residence permits a person of this nationality can apply for, fee, waiting time, documents, eligibility, and which other countries let holders of each one in". The passport index from the benefits route says which countries the person can already enter.
- Budget, if given. Available funds, if the person asked to include them (run the proof of funds route first).

## Screen each option

From the excerpts, list each option with country, fee, waiting time, nationality limits and required documents. Drop an option when:

- the person already holds a valid document of that type from that country;
- their nationality is excluded or not on its "only for" list;
- the page says it is closed, paused or discontinued;
- its fee, or its minimum investment, is above the budget given, or above the available funds when the person asked to include them;
- it is a visa for a country the person can already enter visa-free, unless the user wants it for its effects elsewhere.

Mark an option Needs checking when its eligibility, age limit or proof-of-funds rule cannot be confirmed from the dossier, or the excerpts do not state it.

## Score each remaining option

1. New destinations: the countries whose excerpts say holders of this option may enter, that are not already unlocked for the person, plus the issuing country itself. A visa for a shared visa area adds every member the person cannot already enter (the research answer names the members). Count only rules whose other conditions the person meets or might meet (Needs checking counts as a half). Say the count covers only what the pages researched today state.
2. Cost: the fee, in its currency; convert only with a rate the user gives. Unknown fee: mark the cost unknown and rank the option after the ones with known fees.
3. Time: the processing time on the page. Unknown: treat as 30 days and say so.
4. Value: new destinations per 100 units of the fee currency, and new destinations per week of waiting.

Rank by new destinations, then by value, then by shortest waiting time. Show the top five, each with what it unlocks, fee, waiting time, required documents checked against the dossier (have, missing, too old), and the page address with its fetched date.

## Sequence

When two or more options are worth getting, order them:

1. Options that unlock other options first, for example a visa that makes the person eligible for an e-visa elsewhere.
2. Then the shortest processing time.
3. Never two applications that hold the passport at the same time; when the page says the passport is kept during processing, schedule it alone.
4. Check every passport has at least the validity the options need on the expected issue date; if not, renew the passport first.

Never promise approval. Offer to prepare the chosen option: the plan route puts its application in the trip queue.
