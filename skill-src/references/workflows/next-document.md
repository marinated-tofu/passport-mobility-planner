# Next document route

Answers "which visa, authorisation or residence permit should I get next to open up the most countries for the least money and waiting time".

## Inputs

- The person's holdings and today's unlocked benefits from the benefits route.
- Every option in the option files.
- Budget, if given. Available funds, if the person asked to include them (run the proof of funds route first).

## Screen each option

Drop an option when:

- the person already holds a valid document of that type from that country;
- their nationality is excluded or not on its "only for" list;
- its name or how-to-apply line says it is closed, paused or discontinued (it stays in the files for people with pending applications);
- its fee, or its minimum investment, is above the budget given, or above the available funds when the person asked to include them;
- it is a visa for a country the person can already enter visa-free (a benefit rule or the passport matrix says so), unless the user wants it for its effects elsewhere.

Mark an option Needs checking when its eligibility, age limit or proof-of-funds rule cannot be confirmed from the dossier.

## Score each remaining option

Each option lists "Also unlocks", the benefit rules it counts towards in other countries. For each option:

1. New destinations: the countries in "Also unlocks" that are not already unlocked for the person, plus the issuing country itself. A Schengen visa adds every Schengen country the person cannot already enter (list in the entry conditions reference). Always show this count for each option; without a passport matrix, say it counts only what the bundled rules confirm. Count only rules whose other conditions the person meets or might meet (Needs checking counts as a half).
2. Cost: the fee in US dollars from the option. Unknown fee: mark the cost unknown and rank the option after the ones with known fees.
3. Time: processing days from the option. Unknown: treat as 30 days and say so.
4. Value: new destinations per 100 US dollars, and new destinations per week of waiting.

Rank by new destinations, then by value per 100 US dollars, then by shortest waiting time. Show the top five, each with what it unlocks, fee, waiting time, required documents checked against the dossier (have, missing, too old), and the source.

## Sequence

When two or more options are worth getting, order them:

1. Options that unlock other options first, for example a visa that makes the person eligible for an e-visa elsewhere.
2. Then the shortest processing time.
3. Never two applications that hold the passport at the same time; when an option says the passport is kept during processing, schedule it alone.
4. Check every passport has at least the validity the options need on the expected issue date; if not, renew the passport first.

Never promise approval. Say the score counts benefits written in the bundled rules only.
