# Expiry route

Lists every document in the household that expires in the next 12 months, soonest first.

For each document show the holder, document type, issuing country, expiry date and days left. For passports also show two earlier dates that matter more than the printed expiry:

- six-month date: expiry minus six months, after which countries with a six-month validity rule can refuse entry;
- three-month date: expiry minus three months.

When a passport, visa or residence permit unlocks benefits (benefits route), list the benefits lost on its expiry.

When the passport is the item to renew, say what a new passport means for the documents tied to the old one, and point to each issuing country's official page to confirm:

- visas in the old passport: many countries, the United States among them, keep a valid visa valid in an expired passport, and the traveller carries both passports; others require a new visa or a transfer;
- residence permits and resident ID cards: the residence record usually has to be updated with the new passport details, as in the Gulf states, before the next exit or renewal;
- bookings, travel authorisations and applications made with the old passport number: most must be updated or made again.

If nothing expires within 12 months, say so and give the next expiry date in the household.

Renewal times and fees vary by country and change often; do not state them. Point to the issuing country's entry in the official sources reference.
